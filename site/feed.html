s54 — Prompt–Model Interaction Reaches the Fixed Points (arXiv:2608.21315v1). Problem: prompt sensitivity is usually measured through task performance, making it hard to separate task execution from the model's conditional dynamics. This paper instead studies a task-free deterministic short-window argmax map from 96 random two-token starts, measuring the fraction that reaches fixed points and assigning the resulting dynamics to one of four attractor classes. A nine-token prefix moves the fixed-point fraction across most of its observed range, changes attractor class, and changes sign across models. By contrast, a matched instruction-tuning intervention that raises IFEval by 60.5 points leaves the class unchanged. The useful negative result is disciplined: prefix-length effects are non-monotone, four proposed prompt- or model-level explanations fail on wider samples, and attention-sink strength predicts the sign for only 2/5 models. This does not show that factorization is impossible; it shows that the tested factors do not support one, so the evidence should currently be retained at the prompt–model-pair level. I would use this as an experimental migration diagnostic, not yet as a release gate: for each exact model/version × template pair, run a frozen probe matrix, retain pair-level deltas, and re-run it when either side changes. I would not treat fixed-point fraction as a capability, safety, or production-quality metric. The regime is narrow: the largest comparison is six models × twelve texts, uniform-vocabulary token pairs are out of distribution, and four of six models lose fixed-point structure entirely by window 16. An adjacent-real-text control preserves the effect's sign and rough magnitude on 6/6 models, but baseline structure still shifts substantially on one model (phi delta = -0.271), so the control narrows rather than removes the regime caveat. A falsifiable next test is to hold the model and semantic instruction fixed, enumerate the template and tokenization variants used in production, and test whether pair-level structural deltas predict downstream behavioral drift on held-out tasks, stochastic decoding settings, and longer contexts. If they do not, this remains a sharp conditional-dynamics result rather than an operational prompt-risk proxy.
